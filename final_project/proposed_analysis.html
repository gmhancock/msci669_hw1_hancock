Brief Project description:

This analysis is for a 2x2x2 factorial design experiment where I am testing the effects of Oxygen (high and low), Temperature (high and low), and duration of exposure (acute 5 days and prolonged 3 weeks) on a number of whole organism responses and tissue level changes. Because I have so many response variables, I have chosen two that will help build a frame work for the rest; CTmax (thermal tolerance) and RVM (relative ventricular mass) The CTmax values were measured twice on the same fish, while RVM is not repeated on individuals since it is a different end point. Another difference between these two variables are the sample sizes. Repeated measures (CTmax) were tested on 12 individuals per treatment (4 treatments of oxygen and temperature) and the same individuals were tested twice (acute and prolonged time point)

Lethal measures were taken from 6 unique individuals for each treatment/duration combination with an additional time point of a one day exposure (turns into a 2X2X3, with 4 environmental conditions sampled 3 times=12 sampling events total). the added time level changes my analysis because the time between day 1, day 5, and week 3 are not equally distributed and I want to incorporate the actual duration of exposure into my analysis not just a comparison between two categories like I will for the first one. The RVM/lethal measures are further complicated by their relation to the “control” treatments at each time point. The lethal measures for the three stress exposure treatments (high temp, low oxygen, high temp + low oxygen) were sampled at n=6 at the three time points as described. Due to limitations in equipment and time, we had to stagger the days these lethal samples (and the whole organism measures) were taken, meaning all of the stress conditions were sampled with the same exposure durations, but on different dates. To account for this, we sampled the control treatment (which was never ramped, and remained at the pre experiment stable conditions for the whole time) at a higher frequency so that every stress treatment sample had a control replicate. This allows us to see if day sampled/over all time in captivity impacted our findings.

This plot used raw data for treatments and controls, but really I want to calculate the difference from the control. below I am comparing the difference between mean control and treatment RVM for each sampling date with 95% CI in the bars. I used AI to help make this plot

rvm_diff <- plot_data %>%
  group_by(Date, Treatment) %>%
  summarise(
    RVM = mean(`RVM (%)`, na.rm = TRUE),
    SE = sd(`RVM (%)`, na.rm = TRUE) / sqrt(sum(!is.na(`RVM (%)`))),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) %>%
  group_by(Date) %>%
  mutate(
    Control = RVM[Treatment == "Control"][1],
    Control_SE = SE[Treatment == "Control"][1],
    Difference = RVM - Control,
    SE_diff = sqrt(SE^2 + Control_SE^2),
    Lower = Difference - 1.96 * SE_diff,
    Upper = Difference + 1.96 * SE_diff
  ) %>%
  filter(Treatment != "Control")

ggplot(rvm_diff, aes(x = Date, y = Difference, color = Treatment)) +
  geom_hline(yintercept = 0, linetype = "dashed") +
  geom_errorbar(aes(ymin = Lower, ymax = Upper), width = 1) +
  geom_point() +
  geom_line() +
  labs(
    x = "Date",
    y = "Difference from Control RVM (%)"
  ) +
  theme_classic()
